# Human review gates

## Purpose

Human review is a control for decisions where linguistic evidence is incomplete, conflicting, high-risk, or consequential for later generation. It is not a cosmetic approval step.

## Scope of these gates

The human review gates described here apply to lexical evidence and the dictionary-development pipeline. They do **not** mean that every output in the planned 1,920-output confirmatory benchmark will receive independent human review.

## Gate conditions

A candidate should be held for review when it has an unresolved sense distinction, competing target realizations, a possible construction shift, an unsupported frequency or register claim, conflicting validators, or a failed required check.

## Decision states

A reviewer can accept, request repair, reject, defer, or mark a case as needing broader adjudication. The decision should identify the reviewed evidence and why it is sufficient or insufficient. A deferred case does not become validated by elapsed time.

## Separation of roles

Generation, validation, and adjudication should be independently reviewable. This does not require perfect independence in every setting, but prevents a single model output from serving as proposal, proof, and approval.

Model proposals are not automatically promoted to verified lexical truth.

## Auditability

A public study should preserve the routing rule, evidence presented to a reviewer, decision outcome, and later correction. Personal reviewer information is not required for public reporting.
